# Telecom Customer Churn & Revenue Analysis

This project analyzes customer growth, recurring revenue, customer engagement, support interactions, subscription characteristics, and churn behavior for a simulated telecommunications company.

The analysis uses **Python, SQL (SQLite), and Tableau** to identify trends in customer retention and revenue and translate those findings into business insights and recommendations.

## Project Status

The analysis and Tableau dashboards are complete. The project includes analysis of:

- Monthly active customers
- Monthly recurring revenue (MRR)
- Average revenue per user (ARPU)
- Customer additions
- Customer churn
- Net customer growth
- Churn by customer segment
- Churn by subscription plan
- Churn by contract type
- Churn by support-ticket activity
- Churn by ticket volume and resolution status
- Churn by customer demographics
- Churn by data usage
- MRR by customer segment
- MRR by subscription plan
- Usage trends leading up to churn
- Consolidated churn-risk indicators
- Business recommendations based on the analysis

## Key Areas of Investigation

The analysis focuses on two primary business questions:

1. **How is the customer base and recurring revenue performing?**

2. **What customer characteristics and behaviors are associated with churn?**

The project examines both high-level business KPIs and detailed customer-level patterns to develop a clearer understanding of factors associated with customer retention.

## Key Findings

The analysis found that the customer base and recurring revenue grew throughout most of the year, but net customer growth weakened substantially toward the end of the year as churn increased.

Several customer characteristics and behaviors were associated with higher churn, including:

- Month-to-month contracts
- Higher support-ticket volume
- Unresolved support issues
- Lower average data usage
- Certain subscription plans

The analysis also examined customer demographics and usage behavior to determine whether they showed meaningful relationships with churn.

These relationships are presented as **associations rather than causal conclusions**.
